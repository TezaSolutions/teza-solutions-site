What Uber Eats really costs you — the full breakdown

Uber Eats, DoorDash, SkipTheDishes. These platforms take between 15% and 30% on every order they process. That percentage stays abstract — until you convert it into dollars.

What you hand over every month, by volume

Here is the raw math, unrounded.

$5,000 monthly volume

  • At 15%: $750 in commissions
  • At 25%: $1,250 in commissions
  • At 30%: $1,500 in commissions

$10,000 monthly volume

  • At 15%: $1,500 in commissions
  • At 25%: $2,500 in commissions
  • At 30%: $3,000 in commissions

$20,000 monthly volume

  • At 15%: $3,000 in commissions
  • At 25%: $5,000 in commissions
  • At 30%: $6,000 in commissions

These add up. A restaurant doing $10,000 in platform orders hands over, on average, $24,000 to $36,000 a year in commissions alone.

What that money buys instead

Placed back into your daily operation, those amounts look like this:

With $1,500 a month, you cover:

  • A part-time employee's monthly wage (15 hrs/week at minimum wage)
  • Or an entry-level commercial convection oven
  • Or three months of a full POS system

With $3,000 a month, you cover:

  • A full-time employee's monthly wage at minimum wage
  • Or a backup walk-in cooler
  • Or your entire supplier invoice for some weeks

With $5,000 a month, that's:

  • Two part-time employees
  • Or a partial kitchen renovation spread over 12 months
  • Or a full year of outsourced bookkeeping

This isn't a criticism of the platform model. It's simply what the numbers say once you put them in context.

What changes when you take those sales direct
